What you'll actually do

Prospect & CRM support

targets · segments · fit

Build and maintain high-quality target lists — retreat facilitators, architects, wellness operators, luxury curators, creative directors, boutique agencies.

  • Review, add, or correct prospects
  • Tag by segment, geography, aesthetic fit
  • Remove bad-fit or low-quality contacts

Outreach batch review

before anything sends

Before any email goes out, you check it — not write it from scratch. Your value is curation and taste. Nothing sends that you haven't been able to change (§07).

  • Good fit? Tone aligned, not salesy?
  • Anything factually wrong?
  • Skip, rewrite, personalize, or approve?

Outreach inbox management

no good reply gets lost

You manage the outreach inbox day to day — under whichever signature §06 lands on.

  • Check + classify incoming replies
  • Flag warm leads and important opportunities
  • Mark irrelevant / negative, suggest next step

Field intelligence from the house

what the Toolkit can't see

Because you're on-site, you ground the whole GTM process in what's actually true.

  • Observations, guest reactions, photos/video
  • Campaign ideas from what's happening on site
  • What feels true or false in the messaging

Social monitoring

every channel · the 24-hour clock

People find us in public long before they reach an inbox. You watch the channels and decide what's worth pulling out.

  • @chamana.live — comments, mentions, tags, story replies, and the DM requests folder, where good people get filtered and never seen
  • lachamana.com — blog comments, forms, newsletter signups
  • New channels join by default. TikTok and YouTube are ad platforms today, not accounts we post on
  • Good-fit accounts into the CRM · which posts pull the right people

One timing thing: once someone DMs us, Instagram gives 24 hours to reply on the official path. The rest of the week can wait — that can't.

Routing is the same as §04: a booking or a press mention goes to Iru, tone goes to René.

Content curation

choosing, not writing

You help decide what goes out, in what order, and whether it's good enough.

  • Review the publish-ready packs — caption, hero image, suggested date — before they go anywhere
  • Curate the asset library in Drive (02_Content/Assets/): what's approved, what's tired, what's missing
  • Sequence the calendar across channels, including what shouldn't go out
  • Tell us what the house can supply: "we need photos of X"

Voice and aesthetic sign-off stay with René, and articles publish as La Chamana, never a person. The machine stops at a finished draft — someone posts it by hand, today Iru.

Worth knowing before you open Instagram: get into @chamana.live from the phone app only.

A desktop login, a new IP or a VPN has locked that account before, and it now has live ad spend behind it. So no instagram.com in a browser, and nothing third-party asking for the password.

Posting and scheduling go through Meta Business Suite, which is fine — just a different door. Anything beyond the phone, ping me and we sort it in five minutes.

06

The send identity

open decision

Your note: "Lara should be Bianca para no pasear a los contactados."

Agreed on the substance. If you're the person who answers, you should be the person who signs. That's not a softer position than the current one — it's a stricter one, and it matches a rule the system already has: nothing we send may imply a human wrote it personally when none did. Signing with a real name that a real person stands behind removes the problem instead of managing it.

Why it isn't done tonight. "Lara" isn't a name in a document — it's a live mailbox with its own sending reputation, its SMTP setup, the sequence tooling, the DM channel, and, since 02-08, the byline on long-form content. Swapping it is a migration.

1 · A new mailbox has no reputation

Your address starts from zero and needs a slow first week. The existing ≤20 sends/day ceiling already puts us at that pace, so this costs days, not weeks.

2 · Contacts already touched

Some people already got something signed Lara — the reply desk is holding live threads. Those don't restart from zero; they get one honest handover line. Exact count comes Tuesday.

3 · Outreach vs. byline

Two decisions, not one. Proposal: outreach signs Bianca; the content byline stays as-is until René weighs in. Don't couple them.

4 · It's your name

A real name on cold outreach is your public face in that channel. That's yours to say yes to, not something to inherit from a document.

Until this is decided on Tuesday: no new cold sends go out. This week is curation, not launching. Replies to existing threads continue as normal.

07

Editing the texts

a real gap

Your note: "Habilitaría una opción de edición directa de todos los textos en general o en su defecto una box de sugerencias."

This is the most useful thing you flagged, and the honest answer is that it doesn't exist. The review screen has no edit field at all. Every message body is a text file, one per prospect, and today only I can touch them. That's a hole in the product, not a decision about how much you're trusted.

Two ways to close it — and yes, they're the two you named:

A · Edit in place

what you type is what sends

An edit box on every row of the review screen. You rewrite the line, hit approve, and that exact text goes out. No round trip, no interpretation.

The right answer. More to build.

B · Suggestion box

you write the change, I apply it

A free-text note per message — tone, a fact to fix, a rewrite. It reaches me, I apply it before the batch releases.

The faster answer. Weaker, because it keeps a human in the middle.

Tuesday decides which one gets built first. Meanwhile the rule holds regardless: every batch reaches you in a form you can change, and nothing sends that you haven't had the chance to rewrite.
