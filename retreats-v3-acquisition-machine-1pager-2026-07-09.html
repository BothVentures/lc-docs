The 3-campaign intent-fence

That is a feature — we do not loosen targeting to clear a budget ceiling.

05

The conversion ladder

the #1 lever
Google has zero lead signal today.

The on-site tag measures store checkout, not the inquiry form — so the bidder is flying blind. Tiers 1–2 are free and safe (they add no new on-site tag; they define events in analytics we already run and import them), and they reach enough volume to move the bidder off "cheapest clicks" within weeks — not the months a raw form-submit trickle would take. This is the single biggest performance lever in the whole scale-up.

06

The money layer

budget · blended cost per booking

The knob Gastón turns. Set one weekly envelope — his example, $500/week — and split it per campaign, by hand or auto. Every approval shows the money it commits and the money at stake.

Blended cost per booking is the metric, not per-platform attribution (every platform double-counts the same conversion). The formula is deliberately simple:

total spend ÷ total conversions = blended CPA

Today: $14.72 ÷ 0 = undefined. That is the honest state — and exactly what the conversion ladder makes real.

The guardrail. Keep acquisition cost under ~15% of booking value. On a 3-night full-house booking ($13,500 floor), that is up to ~$2,025 of ad cost per booking and still inside the rule.

The instinct. If cost per booking sits well under booking value, spend more. In the base case, a booking costs roughly $1,100 in Google spend modeled · see §07 — comfortably under the guardrail, and that's before Meta and outreach stack on top.

Honest edge: at the conservative end, Google-only can run over the guardrail on its own — which is the whole argument for the machine being multi-channel, not one platform.

08

Budget ramp + seasonality

build in the trough · lean into the peak

The timing thesis. Tulum search demand is down materially from its 2021 post-COVID peak (sargazo + security perception) and is strongly seasonal — peaking December and early January. Gastón's observation · refined in the seasonality brief Relative trend matters more than absolute volume. Retreat leaders plan months ahead, so booking-intent leads the attendee peak — which means we build and learn now, in the trough, and lean budget into the autumn planning window that feeds Dec/Jan retreats.

Target channel mix over time: Meta 60–70% / Google 30–40% target. The core spends only ~$3–7/day regardless — Google Search is the high-intent net, Meta carries the volume once its lead-gen build goes live, and seasonality is a Google-search effect more than a Meta one.

Why we're bullish

the thesis in full
TL;DR

The durable asset is not a Google campaign. It is a budget-in → approve → push machine that scaffolds a full campaign from one approval, spends toward a measured cost per booking, and gets smarter with every lead — across every channel. V3 is its first working proof.

Honest on the channel. Google Search demand for Tulum is thin, down materially since 2021, and concentrated in a Dec/Jan window. Meta — the intended volume channel — isn't running at volume yet: the restriction lifted, but the lead-gen build is pending its execution gates. Neither of those is the story.

Bullish on the system. A single booking is worth $13,500+. A machine that turns one weekly envelope into qualified inquiries — and learns which channel, which campaign, which term produces the cheapest real booking — only needs a trickle to pay back many times over. Retreats proves the loop; then Meta's lead-gen build goes live and brings the volume, TikTok and outreach and retargeting stack on top, and the same machine runs every channel. That is the thing worth building — and it is now built enough to watch it work.
